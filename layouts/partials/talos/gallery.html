Kickoff brief · pre-read

A competitive-intelligence platform

Reconstructed and reworded. The first target, names, dates and thresholds are removed.

01 / Problem

Every competitive-intelligence question is answered by hand. Someone stitches the answer together from several disconnected tools, and the next time the same question comes up the evidence trail is stitched together again. Landscape-shape questions absorb the cost through patient analyst effort. Others, such as what competitors have exemplified for a target or what new intellectual property has appeared recently, are effectively unanswerable: the raw filings and hand-kept trackers exist, the answers do not.

The platform makes provenance a property of the substrate. An answer assembled once can be drawn many times, and a record one reader builds is a record every reader can rely on.

02 / Primary user

The competitive-intelligence analyst. The platform is designed against that person's daily workflow: open a Program, read what changed, drill into the evidence behind a claim, export a brief when leadership asks. Chemistry, intellectual property and leadership readers consume the output. They are the next audience, not the first.

03 / Questions the product answers

Analyst level · first release

  • What has surfaced for this target since the last review, with full provenance?
  • Which Programs have moved phase, changed sponsor or surfaced new data?
  • Where is entity recognition uncertain, and what is the analyst's call?
  • What catalysts are coming for tracked Programs?

Leadership level · later

  • Which targets attract the most competitive activity, and what shape does it take?
  • Which Programs show signals worth a partnership conversation?
  • How does the landscape change our read on probability of success?
04 / Requirements
Six requirements, each with how we know it works
#A reader can…We know it works when…
R1Trace any claim to the Event behind it and the Source that reported it, with publish, ingest and revision dates all readable.An analyst can show a skeptical reader where a claim came from without leaving the platform.
R2See one consolidated record when several Sources report the same event, naming every reporting Source and keeping each one's provenance.Two analysts asking the same question reach the same record and the same supporting Sources.
R3See who made any entity match, on what evidence and at what confidence, and confirm or reject uncertain ones."Why this match?" is answered in the platform, and unresolved matches arrive as work an analyst can finish.
R4Navigate by Program, with a record that persists as evidence accrues and keeps prior states.An analyst can answer "what changed in my watchlist last week?" from the platform alone.
R5Read a confidence signal on every claim, in proportion to the evidence behind it."How sure is this?" is visible wherever a claim appears.
R6Export analyst output to a leadership-ready format with provenance preserved.A leadership reader can click any exported claim back to its Source.
05 / Bets and invalidators

Each bet names the behaviour that would force a pivot, and the earliest sign of it.

B1

Programs are the right unit.

Analysts and downstream readers think in Programs, so everything is organized around that unit.

Invalidated if Stakeholders consistently start from another unit, such as phase, modality or sponsor, and find Program navigation gets in the way.

Earliest signal Analysts ask to list everything in one phase before they ask for a particular Program.

B2

Analysts adopt it as their primary tool.

Provenance, confidence and persistent Program records earn it the first-open spot.

Invalidated if Well after delivery, weekly review cycles still do not use it as the primary source.

Earliest signal The platform is opened for demonstrations only.

B3

Honest confidence earns trust.

Leadership will tolerate, and ideally value, a platform that says "we do not know" or "low confidence, one Source".

Invalidated if Leadership routes around it for "definitive" answers and uses it only when convenient.

Earliest signal A reader asks for a flat answer, ignoring the confidence signal, more than once.

06 / Scope edges

In v1

  • A landscape-shape question on one Program, for a first target
  • The analyst as primary user
  • Export to a leadership-ready format, provenance kept1
  • Active Events from canonical Sources, consolidated
  • Program-centered navigation with prior states kept
  • A review queue for uncertain entity matches

Out of v1

  • Rollout to more targets
  • Compound exemplification from patents
  • Ingesting paid third-party analyst reports
  • Conference posters and abstracts
  • Rendering of forward catalysts

Adjacent

  • A commercial dataset compared against, protected and display-only
  • An upstream feed, read-only
  • Platform and environment provisioning
  • Patent extraction for non-intelligence uses

1 The later project plan moves export to a follow-on release (see Delivery planning).

What to look for Solid boxes exist in the application and the dashed box is a documented design that is not built. Every arrow is a move an analyst actually makes, and the event page is where the provenance walk lives.

View map

  • Command search reaches any Program or Event from every view.
  • Cockpit links to Programs index, Landscape, and, by a dashed route, to the Review workbench for uncertain matches.
  • Programs index and Landscape each link to the Program page.
  • Program page links to Event page and to Briefs.
  • Landscape links to Briefs.
  • Event page links to Briefs, and, by a dashed route, to the Review workbench for an uncertain entity.
  • Solid box: built
  • Dashed box: documented design
  • Solid arrow: navigation
  • Dashed arrow: route to a designed view

Data spine

  • Source reports a Document. A Document holds a snippet. The Assertion annotates the snippet. Assertions consolidate into an Event. Events accrue onto a Program.
  • Canonical entities (Organization, Compound, Indication, Target, Trial) are recognized in the Assertion, and a Program joins two or more of them.

Provenance is reached through the chain, with no shortcut to a single "primary source". Confidence is one of three bands, never a number.

02 / Four workstream lanes, by role

Source operations

Decides what the platform pays attention to and how often: source contracts, ingestion, when a Source is adopted or retired.

Recognition

Decides which entity a piece of evidence refers to: the canonical catalog and the resolution streams.

Semantics and output

Decides what an observation means: schema, consolidation, the brief contract and export shape.

Analyst surface

Owns the surfaces an analyst uses end to end: Program pages, landscape, entity pages, briefs and exports.

04 / The evidence packet

A small set of real examples, each chosen to force an architecture decision. Sub-projects 1 and 2 produce it, a working group ratifies it, and the later sub-projects build against what it settled.

  1. Snippet or whole document? A press release whose headline and fourth paragraph contradict each other.
  2. Where does an assertion end and an event begin? One article that reports several events.
  3. Which source types ship first? Chosen from a longer candidate list.
  4. What does confidence look like? One high, one borderline and one ambiguous example.
  5. What does a consolidated event show? Every reporting link, or one canonical link.
