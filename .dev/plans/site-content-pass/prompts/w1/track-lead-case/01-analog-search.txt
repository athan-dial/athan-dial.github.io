THE THING THAT MAKES THIS CASE GOOD — do not lose it:

The obvious version is "manual two-day process became a fast tool." That version is weaker than the
truth. The record shows a real measured performance improvement after an implementation change AND,
months later, the primary expert user reporting that the tool returned FEWER results than a
commercial alternative and wanting more coverage. Athan's own reading on the record was that this was
a perception problem about completeness rather than a relevance problem, and that chasing parity with
an external service was the wrong response — the right one was giving users threshold control and
showing results in tiers so they could see the edge of the match themselves.

THAT TENSION IS THE CASE STUDY. A product that is measurably better and still not trusted by its
expert user is a far more interesting product-judgment artifact than a success metric, and it is the
plan's thesis exactly: what should the tool decide, and what should the expert? Do not bury it in
"what I would change now." It belongs in the BLUF and it should shape the whole piece.
