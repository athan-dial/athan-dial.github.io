# Athan Dial: Website Redesign and Rebrand Plan

## The decision in one page

The site needs a strategic reset, not another visual reskin.

The current presence contains good raw material: a credible portrait, a technically simple Hugo foundation, a stronger-than-average About page, a body of practical AI notes, and several candidate work stories. But it currently presents four adjacent identities:

- data-science leader,
- decision-science practitioner,
- applied-AI operator,
- and tool-heavy Claude Code publisher.

All four are true. None should be the brand by itself.

The redesigned site should present one coherent identity:

> **Athan is an AI product leader for expert work. He builds the shared systems that make expert judgment more powerful without unnecessarily turning that judgment into software.**

The public-facing version should be simpler:

> **I build AI and data products for people whose work depends on judgment.**

Everything on the site should substantiate that sentence through work, writing, and working principles.

The redesign should therefore:

1. make the positioning unmistakable within ten seconds;
2. move validated work—not social-media embeds—to the center of the homepage;
3. unify the portfolio and publication into one visual and conceptual system;
4. distinguish cornerstone thinking from tactical notes;
5. remove or quarantine unverified claims before they undermine trust;
6. retain Hugo and GitHub Pages, while simplifying the source and deployment model;
7. eventually move to a custom domain, without making the domain migration a blocker for the redesign.

## The current site is polished, but it is not making the strongest argument

### What is already working

- The site is calm, professional, and easy to recognize as a senior person's portfolio.
- The portrait adds humanity and immediate credibility.
- The About page contains real voice, especially the transition from research to product work and the phrase “a strong allergy to metric theater.”
- The Hugo site is a sensible technical foundation for a writing- and case-study-heavy presence.
- “The Agency” proves there is real practitioner output behind the applied-AI claim.
- The repository contains enough candidate material to build a serious work portfolio once the evidence is validated.

### What is weakening the brand

| Problem | Why it matters | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The hero says “Data Science · Product · Applied AI” | It names three broad categories without communicating a point of view or distinctive value. | Lead with expert work and the shared layer around it. Use the job title as supporting proof. |
| The primary homepage content is LinkedIn embeds | Two cards render blank in the current capture, and the page sends visitors away before demonstrating anything owned. | Publish native article excerpts and selected work on the site; link outward only as a secondary action. |
| The strongest work is hidden | Case studies exist and remain indexed, but they are absent from navigation because their factual basis is not trusted. | Unpublish first; then replace with two or three evidence-validated case studies. |
| The portfolio and “The Agency” look like different people | One is pale, corporate, and airy; the other is a dark technical zine. The conceptual connection is left for the visitor to invent. | One editorial design system, with controlled variation for essays, notes, and work. |
| “The Agency” is cryptic and sounds commercial | It does not tell a hiring manager what they will find and it creates avoidable employer-safety ambiguity. | Rename the visible destination **Field Notes**. Preserve old URLs with redirects. |
| “Advisory” occupies scarce primary navigation | It foregrounds a secondary activity and can suggest an active consultancy. | Move talks and conversations into About. Keep a simple contact path. |
| The site has contradictory role and evidence claims | The public pages alternately describe the current role as Associate Director, Data Research Lead, and Head of Decision Science & Product. | Establish one verified profile data source used by the homepage, About page, resume, metadata, and PDF. |
| The visual system uses too much empty space and very light type | It feels tasteful, but the page under-signals the density of Athan's thinking and makes longer pages tiring to scan. | Increase information density, body contrast, and section hierarchy. Keep breathing room, lose the acreage. |
| Hidden pages are publicly indexed | The sitemap includes a resume page and seven case studies that the repository itself labels as fabricated or generic. One page still links to “consulting services.” | Treat this as a trust and employer-safety issue, not a future polish item. |

### The first intervention should happen before the redesign

Before visual work begins:

- mark the current case studies as drafts or `noindex`, and remove them from the public sitemap;
- remove or redirect the outdated HTML resume page until every title and metric is verified;
- remove the “consulting services” link and any inconsistent role title;
- replace the two LinkedIn iframes with native cards or temporarily remove the section;
- remove the personal phone number from the public repository unless its publication is intentional;
- choose one canonical source for name, title, bio, email, social links, and resume facts.

The public site currently makes claims the source repository explicitly says should not be trusted. Fixing that is higher priority than choosing fonts.

## The brand should have one center and several proofs

### Working category

**AI product leadership for expert work**

This is broad enough to travel beyond biotech and specific enough to avoid becoming generic “AI product strategy.” It includes scientists, analysts, clinicians, researchers, and other users whose work depends on tacit knowledge and contextual judgment.

### Core proposition

**Athan finds the shared product layer inside complex expert workflows.**

That layer may be an application, a data product, an AI capability, shared infrastructure, a verification mechanism, or a reusable workflow. The important signal is that Athan does not begin with the software form. He begins with the experts, the work, and the boundary between local flexibility and shared reliability.

### Signature question

> **What should experts be able to compose, and what must the product make reliable around them?**

This is a recognizable lens that can connect case studies, essays, talks, and product leadership without reducing every subject to “better decisions.”

### Brand promise

**Build the common structure that makes expert judgment more powerful without flattening the expertise itself.**

### Proof pillars

| Pillar | What it demonstrates | Evidence to feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product judgment | Athan reframes presented solutions, identifies the right intervention, and draws boundaries around what should become a product. | Product-shape decisions, build-versus-compose choices, roadmap framing, adoption work. |
| Technical credibility | Athan can reason with engineers and scientists about data, ML, architecture, evaluation, and verification. | PhD training, compound-search systems, data platforms, predictive workflows, hands-on AI systems. |
| Organizational leverage | Athan turns local capability into shared practice through workflows, standards, governance, and product adoption. | Cross-team platforms, operating models, executive synthesis, AI enablement, institutional knowledge. |

### What moves out of the headline

- **Decision science** remains an important method and proof of rigor, but it is no longer the whole category.
- **Decision velocity with traceability** remains a strong talk or essay idea, not the universal brand line.
- **Applied AI** becomes concrete through expert workflows and organizational adoption rather than standing alone as a fashionable noun.
- **PhD** remains a powerful credential, but it should support the identity instead of appearing in the wordmark as `ATHAN DIAL.PHD`.

### The career signal the site should send

> Give me ambiguous product problems where expert behavior, AI capability, shared data, and organizational adoption collide. I can find the product inside them and lead the work from framing through adoption.

That is the signal future employers should receive without having to reverse-engineer it from a skills list.

## The homepage should behave like a sharp executive brief

### Recommended hero copy

**Eyebrow**  
AI product leadership for expert work

**Headline**  
I build AI and data products for people whose work depends on judgment.

**Supporting copy**  
My work sits between expert workflows, shared infrastructure, and applied AI: what should be standardized, what should stay flexible, and how a promising capability becomes something an organization can trust.

**Credibility line**  
Associate Director, Data Science & Product Management at Montai Therapeutics · PhD-trained data scientist · Boston

**Primary action**  
View selected work

**Secondary action**  
Read current thinking

The resume should remain easy to find in the header and footer, but it should not compete with the site's argument as the main hero action. “Connect on LinkedIn” should not be the first thing the site asks a new visitor to do.

### Homepage sequence

1. **Hero: the category and point of view**  
   One screen, one proposition, two actions, and a smaller portrait treatment than the current half-page headshot.

2. **Selected work: proof before biography**  
   Two or three evidence-validated case studies. Each card should expose the user, the constraint, Athan's role, and what changed—not merely a dramatic title.

3. **The shared layer is where leverage compounds**  
   A compact section explaining the repeated product move across the work: deciding what belongs in shared data, infrastructure, verification, workflow, or interface.

4. **Current thinking**  
   One cornerstone essay and two shorter notes, rendered natively. “Building Around Expert Judgment” should be the first featured essay once published.

5. **From research depth to product leadership**  
   A short trajectory section connecting the PhD, data-science work, product leadership, and current focus. The full biography belongs on About.

6. **A low-pressure conversation path**  
   LinkedIn, email, and resume. No consultancy funnel, discovery-call language, or artificial lead capture.

### What should disappear from the homepage

- embedded LinkedIn iframes;
- a generic skills inventory;
- “Where to next” navigation that repeats the header;
- three equal-weight identity labels;
- giant empty vertical regions;
- decorative counters such as `02 // From LinkedIn` unless the numbering communicates a real sequence.

### URL plan

| Current route | New route | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/case-studies/` | `/work/` | Redirect after validated work is ready. Until then, unpublish and `noindex`. |
| `/agency/` | `/thinking/notes/` or `/field-notes/` | Preserve old deep links with permanent redirects; remove “Agency” from visible branding. |
| `/advisory/` | `/about/#conversations` | Redirect and keep the copy in a smaller, employer-safe context. |
| `/writing/` | `/thinking/` | Merge into the canonical writing destination. |

| `/skills/*` | `/open-tools/*` or archive in place | Keep as a secondary technical surface, not primary brand navigation. |

### Content types

- **Work**: substantial, evidence-validated product narratives.
- **Essays**: original point-of-view pieces that develop Athan's intellectual territory.
- **Field notes**: concise practitioner observations, patterns, and recoveries from real work.
- **Open tools**: reusable skills, playbooks, and code-facing documentation.
- **Talks and conversations**: topic list and selected appearances, housed under About or Thinking.

This structure separates intellectual importance from production format. A two-minute Claude Code recovery note should not receive the same visual weight as a cornerstone essay about expert agency.

## The Work section should prove the thesis without exposing what should stay private

### Launch with fewer cases

The launch target is two strong case studies and one shorter proof note. Seven generic cases dilute trust; two defensible cases create it.

Candidate stories already visible in the broader brand work include:

1. **From a 40-plus-day sourcing cycle toward a 14-day operating target**  
   Strong because it combines expert workflow, product framing, cross-functional coordination, and measurable operational leverage.

2. **Making a three-billion-compound search space usable in scientific work**  
   Strong because it connects ML/data scale to a real expert interface and demonstrates that technical possibility is not the same as product value.

3. **Finding the shared layer beneath local scientific workflows**  
   A shorter case about similarity or substructure search, enumeration tooling, knowledge systems, or another repeated need that became shared capability.

These are working directions, not publication-ready claims. Each must pass the evidence and employer-safety gate below.

### Case-study template

Each case should be scannable in ninety seconds and rewarding at five minutes:

1. **BLUF**: the problem, Athan's role, the intervention, and the result in 60–90 words.
2. **Who was doing the work**: the specific expert users and their context.
3. **What was already possible**: existing tools, local workarounds, and user capability.
4. **The product boundary**: what became common and reliable; what remained expert-controlled.
5. **The hard choice**: options considered, constraint that mattered, and why one path won.
6. **Athan's ownership**: what he directly owned, what he influenced, and who carried adjacent responsibilities.
7. **What changed**: verified adoption, cycle-time, quality, or workflow evidence, plus limitations.
8. **What he would change now**: one candid reflection that shows judgment is still developing.

### Evidence and employer-safety gate

No case study ships until every factual claim is classified:

- **Public and verified**: safe to publish with a source or internal confirmation.
- **Publishable in a range**: exact number redacted, directional evidence retained.
- **Publishable as mechanism only**: explain the decision and system without program names or metrics.
- **Private**: useful for interviews, not for the public site.

The gate should specifically verify:

- role title and dates;
- whether a metric is measured, estimated, or a target;
- whether the result can be attributed to Athan's intervention;
- names of internal programs, partners, models, and vendors;
- dollar figures and portfolio information;
- whether an outcome occurred or was merely projected;
- whether the employer would consider the detail confidential.

Where public proof is thin, use sanitized diagrams, decision artifacts, interface structures, or before/after workflow maps. Mechanism is better than invented precision.

## Thinking should be the intellectual spine, not a social-feed mirror

### Three levels of writing

| Level | Purpose | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cornerstone essays** | Define the territory and the claims Athan wants associated with his name. | Building Around Expert Judgment; judgment becoming more valuable as production gets cheaper; verification in expert AI systems. |
| **Practice essays** | Show how the viewpoint changes product work. | Identifying the shared layer, productizing prototypes, evidence hierarchies, adoption around expert workflows. |
| **Field notes** | Demonstrate active practice and technical proximity. | Read-only automation, delegation, memory across sessions, research with citations, failure-recovery notes. |

### Publishing rule

The website should hold the canonical full text. LinkedIn becomes distribution, not infrastructure.

- Render article excerpts from local Markdown.
- Link to the LinkedIn version when useful, but never depend on an iframe.
- Add author, date, reading time, canonical URL, and Open Graph image.
- Keep taxonomy small: **expert workflows**, **product judgment**, **reliable AI systems**.
- Surface only selected field notes on the main Thinking page; keep the complete archive available but quieter.

### What happens to the current Agency material

Keep the best practitioner material, but change its role:

- “Field Notes” becomes a subsection of Thinking.
- “Playbooks” or “Open tools” remains a technical archive linked from Field Notes and GitHub.
- The dark cyber-technical design is retired or reduced to a subtle variant inside the shared system.
- Generated plugin documentation remains functional at stable URLs but does not define the main personal brand.

The goal is not to hide technical depth. It is to stop technical tooling from becoming the loudest interpretation of Athan's career.

## The visual system should feel like an editorial field guide

### Recommended direction: Editorial Systems

The visual identity should combine the clarity of an executive brief, the texture of a serious essay, and the visible structure of a systems diagram.

It should feel:

- rigorous without looking clinical;
- warm without looking lifestyle-oriented;
- technical without looking like a developer landing page;
- structured without looking like a consulting slide deck;
- personal without becoming a biography site.

### Design language

**Typography**

- Editorial serif for major thesis headings and essay titles: Source Serif 4 or Newsreader.
- Sans serif for body and UI: Manrope can remain if self-hosted cleanly; Inter is an acceptable alternative.
- Monospace only for metadata, evidence labels, and diagrams: IBM Plex Mono or a system mono.
- Body text should be 17–19px with strong contrast and a 65–75 character measure.

**Palette**

- warm paper rather than cold blue-gray;
- deep ink for text;
- deep teal or blue-green as the structural accent;
- restrained amber or copper for evidence, tension, and active states;
- no rainbow of category colors.

Illustrative starting point:

| Role | Color |
| --- | --- |
| Paper | `#F6F2EA` |
| Surface | `#FFFDF8` |
| Ink | `#14202B` |
| Secondary ink | `#53606A` |
| Structural teal | `#17616A` |
| Evidence amber | `#B86B35` |
| Rule | `#D8D1C4` |

These values are direction, not final tokens; they must be tested for contrast.

**Layout**

- a 12-column editorial grid;
- a 1180–1240px maximum shell;
- 680–760px reading columns;
- denser, useful first screens;
- clear section rails, annotations, and evidence labels;
- fewer floating cards, more intentional lines and grouped fields;
- headings that function as compressed claims where the content supports them.

**Imagery**

- one real portrait, treated as editorial supporting material rather than half the product;
- sanitized real artifacts: workflow diagrams, evidence tables, interface crops, system maps;
- no stock photography;
- no AI-generated abstract network art;
- no decorative SVG icon museum.

**Motion**

- minimal and fast;
- restricted to navigation, disclosure, and state changes;
- disabled when `prefers-reduced-motion` is set;
- dark mode deferred until the primary experience is fully polished.

### A visual metaphor worth using carefully

The recurring visual idea can be **boundary and shared layer**: local nodes, a common substrate, and the line at which reliability becomes shared responsibility. Use it in diagrams and section dividers, not as a logo that requires explanation.

## The technical rebuild should simplify the system

### Keep

- Hugo as the content engine;
- Markdown as the source of truth;
- GitHub Pages as hosting;
- a small amount of vanilla JavaScript;
- the existing GitHub identity and redirects.

### Change

1. **Separate source from generated output**  
   Use a `main` source branch and deploy through GitHub Actions to Pages. Stop committing the entire generated `docs/` tree back into the source branch.

2. **Create one profile data source**  
   Name, role, dates, email, social links, biography, and resume metadata should live in one structured file consumed everywhere.

3. **Create explicit content collections**  
   `content/work/`, `content/essays/`, `content/notes/`, and `content/about/`, each with a small validated frontmatter schema.

4. **Add evidence state to work content**  
   Suggested fields: `status`, `evidence_status`, `visibility`, `employer_review`, `canonical_url`, and `featured`.

5. **Decouple generated tool documentation**  
   Keep generated plugin/skills sites at stable subpaths, but build or deploy them as separate artifacts. Do not make every update to a skills release rebuild and recommit the entire personal site.

6. **Remove third-party social embeds**  
   Store approved excerpts locally and link to the original social post.

7. **Preserve redirects**  
   Add a redirect map and automated test so the redesign does not break old Agency, case-study, resume, writing, or advisory links.

### Suggested content model

```yaml
title: "Making a three-billion-compound search space usable"
type: "work"
status: "draft"
evidence_status: "needs-verification"
visibility: "private"
featured: false
role: "Product lead"
users: ["medicinal chemists", "computational scientists"]
themes: ["expert-workflows", "shared-infrastructure"]
summary: "..."
```

The build should exclude anything that is not explicitly `status: published` and `visibility: public`.

## Quality should be part of the brand

### Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA and test it rather than inferring compliance from screenshots.

- semantic heading order and landmarks;
- full keyboard navigation;
- visible focus on every control;
- text contrast of at least 4.5:1 for normal text;
- non-text controls and focus states with sufficient contrast;
- 44px preferred pointer targets;
- descriptive links and alt text;
- reduced-motion support;
- no content that depends on color alone.

W3C's guidance emphasizes focus indicators that are large enough and sufficiently contrasting; the simplest reliable implementation is a clear outline rather than a subtle glow. [W3C: Focus Appearance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html)

### Performance

- no LinkedIn iframes;
- self-host and subset the final fonts;
- responsive AVIF/WebP portraits and artifacts;
- reserve media dimensions to prevent layout shift;
- minimal JavaScript and no client framework;
- lazy-load below-the-fold media;
- compress SVGs and remove unused generated assets.

Use the current Core Web Vitals “good” thresholds as launch gates at the 75th percentile: LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 milliseconds, and CLS at or below 0.1. [web.dev: Core Web Vitals thresholds](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds)

### Search and sharing

- unique title and description for every page;
- canonical URLs;
- `Person`/`ProfilePage` structured data on the profile surface;
- `Article` structured data for essays;
- per-page Open Graph images;
- sitemap containing only public canonical pages;
- Search Console verification and post-launch URL inspection;
- redirects from every retired route.

Google documents `ProfilePage` markup for pages where a person publishes first-hand perspectives and recommends validating the markup and submitting a sitemap. [Google Search Central: ProfilePage structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page)

### Custom domain

Move to a short personal domain after the redesigned site is stable. Keep `athan-dial.github.io` working as the redirecting origin. Verify availability and ownership before choosing the exact domain.

GitHub Pages supports custom domains, HTTPS, and apex/`www` redirects; domain verification should precede DNS changes. [GitHub Docs: custom domains for Pages](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/configuring-a-custom-domain-for-your-github-pages-site/managing-a-custom-domain-for-your-github-pages-site)

## The rebuild should happen in six controlled passes

### Pass 0 — Remove contradictions

**Outcome:** nothing public is known to be outdated, fabricated, employer-unsafe, or visibly broken.

- quarantine case studies and old resume content;
- repair or remove LinkedIn embeds;
- reconcile current title and profile facts;
- remove stale consultancy language and unnecessary personal data;
- preserve a private archive of everything removed.

### Pass 1 — Lock the brand and evidence model

**Outcome:** every page has a job and every public claim has a source state.

- finalize the positioning hierarchy in this plan;
- build the public/private evidence ledger;
- select two flagship work stories;
- select two cornerstone essays;
- decide what Field Notes material remains visible at launch.

### Pass 2 — Create the design target

**Outcome:** one responsive visual direction is selected before implementation.

- create three homepage concepts grounded in **Editorial Systems**;
- compare them at desktop and mobile sizes;
- choose one direction;
- extend it to a work case, essay, and navigation state;
- finalize typography, color, grid, artifact treatment, and accessibility tokens.

### Pass 3 — Build the shared system

**Outcome:** the new shell, content types, deployment, and redirects work with representative content.

- clean branch and deployment architecture;
- shared header, footer, typography, cards, rails, and metadata;
- Home, Work, Thinking, About, and Resume templates;
- structured data, social cards, sitemap, and redirect testing;
- responsive behavior and keyboard states.

### Pass 4 — Migrate only material that earns its place

**Outcome:** the site contains a coherent body of proof, not every artifact ever produced.

- publish two validated cases and one shorter proof note;
- publish the expert-judgment cornerstone essay;
- migrate selected field notes;
- rewrite About and Resume from the shared source of truth;
- archive or redirect the rest.

### Pass 5 — Verify the experience

**Outcome:** a reader can understand, trust, and navigate the site across devices.

- keyboard and screen-reader pass;
- automated accessibility and HTML checks;
- responsive captures at 360, 390, 768, 1280, and 1440px;
- contrast, focus, and reduced-motion checks;
- Core Web Vitals and asset-weight pass;
- broken-link and redirect pass;
- structured-data and social-card validation;
- user test with one hiring leader, one product peer, and one technical/scientific collaborator.

### Pass 6 — Launch and learn

**Outcome:** the site changes the quality of professional conversations.

- launch on GitHub Pages;
- connect the verified custom domain when ready;
- submit sitemap and inspect key URLs;
- monitor only metrics tied to useful behavior;
- schedule a content and claim review every quarter.

## Launch scope should be deliberately small

The first credible release contains:

- one new homepage;
- one Work index;
- two validated case studies;
- one shorter work note;
- one Thinking index;
- one cornerstone essay;
- four to six selected field notes;
- one About page;
- one current HTML resume and PDF;
- working redirects;
- current metadata, social cards, and sitemap.

It does not require:

- dark mode;
- a contact form;
- a CMS;
- article search;
- complex filters;
- animated diagrams;
- a complete migration of every Agency post;
- seven case studies;
- a perfect custom domain on day one.

## Success is a change in the reader's model of Athan

Avoid measuring the rebrand by raw traffic. The useful signals are:

- a hiring leader can accurately describe Athan's category after ten seconds;
- interview conversations begin with product judgment, expert workflows, and system boundaries rather than “tell me about your background”;
- collaborators arrive with a specific idea they want to discuss;
- visitors move from the homepage into Work or a cornerstone essay;
- resume downloads and contact clicks happen after proof consumption, not instead of it;
- people refer to a named idea—expert agency, the shared layer, verification, or what should become product—when explaining why they reached out.

The best version of the site does not merely say Athan has product judgment. It lets the visitor experience that judgment through what is emphasized, what is bounded, what is verified, and what is deliberately left out.
